# SUS + Feedback Survey

Hand this to the participant (or use as a Google Form) immediately after they finish their task scenarios, while the experience is fresh. Everything below "Section A" is participant-facing — copy it as-is into a form. The scoring section at the end is for you, not the participant.

---

## Section A — About you (optional, for describing your sample in the dissertation)

1. Age range: `18-24` / `25-34` / `35-44` / `45-54` / `55+`
2. How would you describe your comfort with using websites/apps? `Not very comfortable` / `Somewhat comfortable` / `Very comfortable`
3. Which role did you just test? `Victim` / `Donor` / `Volunteer` / `Admin`
4. Have you used a similar aid-coordination or emergency-reporting app before? `Yes` / `No` / `Not sure`

## Section B — System Usability Scale (SUS)

For each statement, choose how much you agree, from **1 (Strongly disagree)** to **5 (Strongly agree)**. There are no right or wrong answers — answer based on your experience just now.

| # | Statement | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | I think that I would like to use this system frequently. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 2 | I found the system unnecessarily complex. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 3 | I thought the system was easy to use. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 4 | I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 5 | I found the various functions in this system were well integrated. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 6 | I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 7 | I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 8 | I found the system very cumbersome to use. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 9 | I felt very confident using the system. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 10 | I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |

*(This is the standard, validated System Usability Scale (Brooke, 1986) — don't reword the items, that breaks comparability with published SUS benchmarks.)*

## Section C — Open-ended feedback

1. What did you like most about using the system?
2. What was the most confusing or frustrating part?
3. If you were actually affected by a disaster, would you trust a system like this? Why or why not?
4. Was there anything you expected the system to do that it didn't?
5. Any other comments?

## Scoring the SUS (facilitator-only — not shown to participants)

For each participant:

1. For **odd-numbered items** (1, 3, 5, 7, 9): score = (response − 1)
2. For **even-numbered items** (2, 4, 6, 8, 10): score = (5 − response)
3. Sum all 10 adjusted scores (range 0-40).
4. Multiply by **2.5** → SUS score out of 100.

Average the SUS scores across all participants for your overall result. For context when writing up:

- **Published benchmark:** average SUS score across many studies is ~68 — treat that as "average," not a pass/fail line.
- Scores above ~68 are generally considered above-average usability; above ~80 is considered excellent (Bangor et al., 2008 grading, commonly cited alongside SUS in dissertations — cite this if you use the grading scale).
- Report both the **average SUS score** and the **spread** (min/max or standard deviation) — a single average can hide that, say, admin users scored much higher than victim users, which is itself a useful finding.

Pair the SUS score with your task-completion data (from the facilitator guide's tracking sheet) — task success rate and time-on-task are the quantitative complement to SUS's subjective score, and the two together make a much stronger evaluation chapter than either alone.
